# Raw research: TTS player fidget culture & rituals

*Source pass for [tts-tactile-habits-report.md](./tts-tactile-habits-report.md), sections 1–2.
One bullet per habit/ritual: description, the emotion/motivation behind it, player quotes, source URL. Researched 2026-07-24.*

- **Table flipping as rage expression** — Clicking the "Flip" button physically upends the table and its contents; officially framed as catharsis. Berserk Games' own store copy: "Tabletop Simulator is the only simulator where you can let your aggression out by flipping the table!" and "There are no rules to follow: just you, a physics sandbox, and your friends." Emotion: sanctioned tantrum, a punchline for losing. https://store.steampowered.com/app/286160/Tabletop_Simulator/

- **Table flipping was PLAYER-DEMANDED, not designed** — Devs did not plan it. Developer Kimiko: it was "never something we were going to add in, but the public spoke." Community echo (TRIBALien): table-flipping was added post-launch due to "overwhelming demand" and was "quite literally the most requested feature." Emotion: the culture pushed the physics affordance into the product, not vice versa. https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/46476145506740785/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/622954023421892694/

- **Table flipping as a grind/badge** — Achievements ritualize it: "Rage Quit" = flip the table 100 times; "Ultimate Rage" = flip the table over 1000 times. Players deliberately farm hundreds of flips. Emotion: the tantrum becomes a collectible, a joke you commit to. https://truesteamachievements.com/a78943/rage-quit-achievement and https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1208188076

- **First-session flip spree instead of playing** — Steam reviewer describing their whole purchase experience: "I bought it, flipped the table over and over, and threw stuff around, then closed it out after 30 minutes or so." Emotion: the toybox is the first thing people reach for; the physics IS the onboarding. https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/reviews/

- **Throwing components AT other players** — Not at the board — at the person. "throw my poker chips at whomever I please"; "grab his figurines from his side of the board and throw them back at him." The reviewer explicitly credits this with closing emotional distance during lockdown gaming. Emotion: teasing, affection, physical banter with no body in the room. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/

- **Flinging cards / knocking over chess pieces for effect** — "Cards can be individually flung across the table. Chess pieces can be picked up, knocked over, or tossed aside." Emotion: theatrical move-making; the gesture carries the attitude, not just the game state. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/

- **Stacking vs. flicking chips — the same payout, two moods** — "neatly stack a pool of poker chips to be handed over to the victor, or you can flick each one individually." Emotion: choosing between gracious concession and passive-aggressive slow-drip. Pure ritual; mechanically identical outcomes. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/

- **Sharpie graffiti on the board / on claimed territory** — Reviewer: "taking a sharpie to the board and drawing profanities all over my claimed *Catan* tiles." The built-in drawing tool "allows for you to write on the table and any locked object," with a 10,000-line-per-player cap (a cap that exists because people draw that much). Emotion: territorial marking, shitposting, in-joke annotation. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/ and https://kb.tabletopsimulator.com/game-tools/drawing-tools/

- **Weaponized/absurd component resizing** — Reviewer's friend: "changing the size of the dice to be as big as the table, which knocked over all of our pieces when rolled." Emotion: escalation-for-laughs; breaking the game as a bit. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/

- **Snatching cards out of an opponent's hand and revealing them** — "grabbing my opponent's card out of their hand and revealing it to the table." Emotion: the physical-world taboo you can only do because it's a sandbox; social violation as play. https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/tabletop-simulator-closes-the-gap-of-social-distancing/

- **Shaking the mouse to roll dice — the tactile substitute** — Store copy: "Play just like you do in real life; pick up, rotate, shake, and throw any object." Reviewers: "You shake your mouse to shuffle or roll dice, you can rotate and throw pieces." Player description of the manual method: "gather the dice, shake the mouse back and forth and then release the dice across the table." Emotion: players choose the effortful gesture over the R hotkey because it feels like rolling. https://store.steampowered.com/app/286160/Tabletop_Simulator/ and https://www.shelfside.co/reviews2020/tabletopsimulator-review

- **Dice-roll superstition / re-rolling for feel** — Players distinguish hitting R from hand-shaking, and object to rolls that look wrong even when they're fair: "It's a little disconcerting when the die goes up without rotating and comes down." Advice given is to "press R multiple times for additional randomization." Another player waving off complaints: "that's the beauty if randomness. its... well... random." Emotion: the animation has to LOOK like a legitimate roll or it doesn't count emotionally. https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/3050609385678195829/

- **Cursor hovering as an unintentional tell** — Players read each other's mouse fidgeting like body language. OP: "I don't always want my opponent(s) to see what I'm mousing over since that might telegraph my intentions." Supporting comment: "Imagine pointing at everything you are thinking about. We use mice differently than our hands." OP contrasting with real life: they "don't hover my hand over a particular card until I'm ready" and actively shuffle cards to obscure their thinking. Emotion: the fidget leaks information; hiding cursors has been requested for years and still isn't possible ("There are so many functions in TTS, but such a basic thing is not possible"). https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/371919771763506094/

- **Watching others' hands as a positive social signal** — The flip side, from a review: "Gameplay gets smoothed out as you know what other people are looking at during games as people's hands hover over important cards." Emotion: co-presence; you can tell someone is thinking, so the silence isn't dead air. https://www.shelfside.co/reviews2020/tabletopsimulator-review

- **Flick tool (F6) as skill-shot toy** — Pull back, watch the numbered force line, release. Officially for "Pool, Golf, Shuffleboard, etc.", with docs conceding there are "probably many different ways in which you can use the Flick Tool." Emotion: precision fidgeting; a whole Workshop genre grew around it. https://kb.tabletopsimulator.com/game-tools/flick-tool/

- **Non-game physics toys as a Workshop category** — Tabletop Bowling (40 lanes), Tabletop Pool, Improved Pool (tuned "bounciness, drag"), Tabletop Hockey ("play it like air hockey or make your own rules"), building blocks, Jenga variants, curling, marble obstacle courses. A curated collection exists for "games where flicking or precarious perching of pieces are required." Emotion: people build things whose entire content is the physics — no rules layer at all. https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=810423942

- **Solo idle chaos** — Reader comment on a board-game blog: "I tried to play one game solo on tabletopia when i dicovered it and just ended up throwing cards all over the table." Emotion: the fidget urge fires even with no audience. https://www.meeplemountain.com/articles/board-game-soapbox-i-dont-want-a-physics-simulator-i-want-a-dang-board-game/

- **Gravity slider as a group bit** — Physics options expose a gravity slider ("slide the gravity to 0" for zero-G; the slider "goes twice as high now" for stronger gravity). Emotion: environment-level trolling — change the world rather than any one piece. https://kb.tabletopsimulator.com/host-guides/physics/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/135507780428434306/

- **VR amplifies it into full body language** — In VR "you pick them up and make a throwing motion" to roll; press coverage notes you can "lob a chit at your opponent." Non-verbal cues "like a pointed finger or an exasperated throw of the hands are transmitted effortlessly." Requests exist for holding cards "realistically as you would during a game" and flipping them face down with actual hand movement. Emotion: the fidget culture wants MORE fidelity, not less. https://inairspace.com/blogs/learn-with-inair/tabletop-simulator-virtual-reality-the-ultimate-digital-playground-for-gamers

## Anti-patterns

- **Table flipping by friends becomes harassment** — Original complaint (Karaya, Dec 2014): "Playing a server with some friends and I'm sick of them throwing the table around." Fix: "Host > option > uncheck tableflip." Dev clarification: "the host and any promoted players can flip the table, but regular players won't be able to." One reply's alternative solution: find "less ♥♥♥♥-ish friends." Another asked for a confirmation prompt to prevent ACCIDENTAL flips. https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/627456359718442014/

- **Flick tool feared as a griefing weapon** — Thread OP worried it would become "the favorite tool for trolls that just want to screw games over," calling it "a dangerous weapon in the right hands" and asking it be restricted to promoted players. Counter-argument: "it doesn't matter if they flick everything around or just throw it" — the game is physics-based regardless. Dev (Kimiko): "We give as many options as viable for the host to be able to keep things orderly, but at the end of the day, it's still a sandbox. With that in mind, most likely we'll be adding it to the host options to disable." https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/627457521141310767/

- **Buyers repelled by the chaos-first marketing** — Poster Wh1ppet asked "Is there a mode or settings in the game that can lock everything down and prevent the ability to flip tables, knock pieces over, etc?", saying the game seemed marketed mainly around "flipping tables, destroying games and generally being the troll." Emotion: the fidget culture is load-bearing marketing AND a turn-off for serious players. https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/622954023421892694/

- **The mitigation stack players actually use** — Lock (L key): "Any piece can be locked, and can't be moved until it's unlocked." Semi-Lock (Host Options): objects at rest are "semi-locked in place (can still be picked up but ignores physics)" so nothing is knocked over accidentally. Object weight adjustment to prevent knockovers. Undo/rewind arrows: "there's a convenient backwards and forwards button for the host to click" to restore state after griefing or accidents. Permissions to disable Flip per-player. Emotion: every beloved fidget has a corresponding off-switch, because the same action is delight or vandalism depending on consent. https://kb.tabletopsimulator.com/host-guides/permissions/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/622954023421892694/ and https://www.shelfside.co/reviews2020/tabletopsimulator-review

- **Turning the fidgets off as standard practice for "serious" play** — A how-to guide lists managing permissions specifically to "prevent people from flipping the table" as one of the common annoyances to fix on setup. Table flip "can be disabled by the host for more serious game play." https://entrogames.substack.com/p/tabletop-simulator-104-fixing-common-annoying-parts-of-tabletop-simulator and https://steamcommunity.com/app/286160/discussions/0/46476145506740785/

- **The physics-is-pointless critique** — Meeple Mountain's Kurt Refling on flipping: "if you feel like being a bit of a rascal — a scallywag — a miscreant, even — you can flip the table," but "Tabletop Simulator makes that act hollow and devoid of meaning." On the ergonomic cost of physical manipulation: "Every delicate drag of the mouse, every microscopic maneuver of a victory point marker..." and "Clicking and dragging is a particular scoundrel of mouse use, associated with higher strain on the hand." Reader Patrick: "Being able to toss pieces around adds nothing to almost all board games" and "I'd rather be in VASSAL, but it's easier to get my friends into Tabletop Simulator, so here we are." A BGG-adjacent commenter: it "was made for some viral videos of table flipping and pieces knocking around and then somehow stuck around by inertia." https://www.meeplemountain.com/articles/board-game-soapbox-i-dont-want-a-physics-simulator-i-want-a-dang-board-game/

- **Physics interferes with basic play** — Dice "can be rolled without worrying about crashing into the game board and knocking over pieces" only after adjusting physics settings — implying the default behavior routinely wrecks board state. https://kb.tabletopsimulator.com/host-guides/physics/
