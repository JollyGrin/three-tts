# Raw research: tactile-joy micro-interactions in other digital card/board games

*Source pass for [tts-tactile-habits-report.md](./tts-tactile-habits-report.md), section 4.
Grouped by game: micro-interaction + why it works + source URL, then design-principle takeaways. Researched 2026-07-24.*

## Balatro

- **Hover tilt toward cursor + lift/scale** — Cards rotate on X/Y toward the pointer and rise with a scale bump and shadow when hovered; the hand is in constant micro-motion even when idle. *Why:* the card reads as a rigid physical object being picked at rather than a sprite; it also gives free, no-stakes feedback so the player is "playing" before committing to a move. Widely cited as the canonical example of UI juice that is fun without gameplay. https://www.wavebeem.com/toybox/2025/balatro/ · https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Spring wobble / over-rotation on settle (±~3°)** — Card movements overshoot and spring-bounce rather than lerping to a stop. *Why:* springs imply mass and inertia; linear easing reads as "UI", spring settle reads as "object". https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Selection as a second, higher lift tier** — Hover ≈ translateY(-12px)/scale(1.05); selected ≈ translateY(-24px)/scale(1.08) with intensified shadow and golden glow. *Why:* encodes state (hovered vs committed) purely in physical elevation, no text label, no checkbox. Note: these exact values come from a web recreation/analysis, not from Balatro's source. https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Reorder with simulated inertia + magnetic damping on snap** — Dragging a card in hand pushes adjacent cards aside with physical inertia, then the dragged card snaps into the gap with a damped magnetic feel. *Why:* neighbors reacting to the dragged card is what sells a *hand* rather than a row of buttons; the damped snap gives a satisfying "click into place" without a hard cut. https://medium.com/@yyh19971004/balatro-design-analysis-visual-packaging-and-interactive-feedback-cc6fa6a65370
- **Material-based card editions (Foil, Holographic, Polychrome, Negative, Glass, Steel, Gold, Stone)** with layered shaders — background layer, rainbow/gradient layer, shine/gloss layer, art layer, animated over ~3s cycles and reacting to hover with dynamic lighting. *Why:* the "material" carries mechanical meaning (glass = fragile high-mult, gold = generates money) so decoration doubles as information with zero cognitive cost; parallax between layers implies real depth under a real surface. https://medium.com/@yyh19971004/balatro-design-analysis-visual-packaging-and-interactive-feedback-cc6fa6a65370 · https://www.wavebeem.com/toybox/2025/balatro/
- **Screen shake tiered by score magnitude** — roughly 2–4px/0.2s for minor hands, 4px/0.3s mid, 8px + rotation/0.5s for big scores. *Why:* shake is used as *data*, not garnish — the player feels "that was a big hand" before reading a number. https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Slot-machine digit rolling with staggered per-digit timing** — chip/mult counters roll up rather than snapping to a value. *Why:* stretches a single instantaneous calculation into an anticipation curve; the eye tracks change instead of diffing two static numbers. https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Audio-visual pitch sync** — rising pitch per scored card, "ka-ching" layer on multiplier, bass drop on threshold; the frequency of the jumping numbers is synchronized to the pitch of the audio. *Why:* cross-modal binding — sound and motion arriving on the same beat are perceived as one physical event rather than two effects. https://medium.com/@yyh19971004/balatro-design-analysis-visual-packaging-and-interactive-feedback-cc6fa6a65370
- **Five simultaneous feedback channels on scoring** — card animation, digit roll, screen shake + CRT scanline intensification, color-matched particle burst, layered audio. *Why:* "high-density positive feedback" — compressed multi-sensory stimulus per action. The stated thesis: *feedback is the product, not the polish* — remove the animations and you have a spreadsheet. https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro
- **Unified CRT aesthetic fiction** — scanlines, phosphor glow, curved vignette applied globally. *Why:* effects read as one physical world/screen rather than a stack of separate FX layers; the frame itself is a diegetic object.
- **Adjustable animation speed setting** — juice can be dialed down. *Why:* respects veteran players' agency; juice that can't be skipped becomes friction on the 500th hand. https://medium.com/@yyh19971004/balatro-design-analysis-visual-packaging-and-interactive-feedback-cc6fa6a65370
- **Design-by-feel principle from the developer** — LocalThunk's analogy: hanging a picture, a frame that *feels* level but isn't is better than one technically level that feels askew. *Why:* explicit license to tune animation values perceptually rather than mathematically. https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/
- **Recognition:** won the 2025 Apple Design Award for *Delight and Fun*, awarded specifically on the strength of feedback/interaction quality. https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-unveils-winners-and-finalists-of-the-2025-apple-design-awards/

## Hearthstone

- **Cards waver and drift while in hand** — idle micro-motion on held cards from the very first build. *Why:* the hand is never dead; motion implies the cards are being held by something with a pulse rather than pinned to glass. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Cards slam down onto the board on play** — impact, not placement. *Why:* mimics the real physical pleasure of slapping a card on a table; converts a state change into a percussive event. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Minions transform state on play** — the card object becomes a creature that moves independently, collides with neighbors, and crumbles when destroyed. *Why:* the explicit design goal was "start from the feel of a physical card, then do what paper can't"; the transition earns the digital medium instead of just simulating cardboard. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Attack = card leaps across the board and strikes the target** — physical traversal rather than an arrow or number tick. *Why:* causality is shown as motion; the player sees force transfer. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Board shake on large damage spikes + unseen audience gasp on big summons** — environmental reaction scaled to magnitude. *Why:* the world reacts to the play, implying stakes and an audience; social validation without other players. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Bouncing and over-rotation as a house-wide UI physics rule** — the shop rocks when a pack is selected, chosen packs fall from the sky and impact the board, announcement banners are wooden signs that swing. *Why:* one consistent physics grammar across *all* UI, not just cards, makes the entire interface feel like a room of objects. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Interactive game boards / clickable fidget toys** — 33+ battlefields, each with clickable props that animate and play sound with zero gameplay effect: Orgrimmar's draggable catapult (drag boulders onto it, click repeatedly to re-cock the arm and fire at the opponent), dragon eggs that shatter under rapid clicking and sometimes release a Whelp, gardens you water and harvest, Stormwind's clickable gryphon/collapsing structures/working clock, a statue whose eyes drop rubies when clicked, a bush that launches a fruit you can slice mid-air (Fruit Ninja reference), Pandaria's random crops with a ~1/10,000 golden vegetable. *Why:* the stated rationale is that **players of physical games fiddle with something between turns**, so the fidget behavior was built into the virtual table — it converts dead opponent-turn time into play, and adds discovery/amusement. https://outof.games/news/341-hearthstone-hypothesis-why-do-the-game-board-interactions-exist/ · https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Game_boards
- **Hidden ordered-click secrets** — clicking specific objects in a specific sequence (e.g. Un'Goro's drum/leaf sequence D-G-E-G-A triggering a volcano eruption; Scholomance, Rastakhan, Uldum secrets). *Why:* turns idle fidgeting into a discoverable long-tail reward; gives the toy layer a ceiling. https://outof.games/news/341-hearthstone-hypothesis-why-do-the-game-board-interactions-exist/
- **A tutorial fidget** — the Pandaria gong appears to have been placed deliberately to teach new players that boards are touchable at all. *Why:* affordance discovery — a fidget layer nobody knows exists has no value. https://outof.games/news/341-hearthstone-hypothesis-why-do-the-game-board-interactions-exist/
- **Golden cards: full animated recolor of the static art** — each golden variant hand-animated, prizing detail and large-area motion. *Why:* rarity you can see across the table at a glance; ownership display, and the animation reads as the card being "alive". https://austincurzon.com/hearthstone-golden-card-animations · https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Golden_card
- **Pack opening deliberately simplified to an explosion** — the team rejected an elaborate ribbon-untying/flap-opening sequence: "let's just blow the thing up," cards burst forth in light and color with the orange glow signalling a legendary. *Why:* dramatic impact beat fidelity-to-real-packaging; the reveal moment is what matters, not the simulation. https://toucharcade.com/2017/04/04/designing-hearthstone-card-packs-animations-iterations-ungoro-and-more-with-art-director-ben-thompson/
- **Emotes and heavy voice-line/soundbite library** — heavily worked on by the game director personally. *Why:* constrained expression channel gives social presence and character without chat; soundbites become cultural touchstones/memes, i.e. shared vocabulary. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Warm tavern audio palette** — accordion, penny whistle, Celtic folk tone deliberately chosen against Warcraft's epic register. *Why:* frames the game as a cozy pub table you're sitting at, reinforcing the physical-place fiction. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone
- **Cards chosen over alternatives for the "common language" reason** — the team prototyped voodoo dolls, gear contraptions, magical jars, moonwells, papercraft minions before landing on cards. *Why:* card affordances are pre-learned by everyone; skeuomorphism as zero-cost onboarding. https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone

## Inscryption

- **No conventional UI — all state lives on physical props on the table** — health tracked by a pair of golden scales that physically tip, teeth used as weights, candles, knives, skulls, goat heads that gate card placement. *Why:* fully diegetic state; you read the game by looking at objects, so every "UI update" is an object moving. https://mdshepard1.medium.com/inscryption-how-and-why-bb8d86a68d07 · https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2474/2467/2503
- **First-person seated-at-the-table framing with heavy shadow falloff** — low candlelight, most of the cabin dark, so the world collapses to the table between you and the opponent. *Why:* lighting as focus control and as a physical-presence cue; the table is a place, not a screen region. https://mdshepard1.medium.com/inscryption-how-and-why-bb8d86a68d07
- **You can stand up and leave the table** — walk the cabin, open a safe, solve painting/cabinet puzzles, find items that come back to the table as cards or permanent modifiers. *Why:* the card game exists inside a physical space rather than being the whole universe; the ability to *stop playing and touch something else* is itself the fidget layer, and discoveries feed back into the game. https://entertainium.co/2021/10/18/inscryption-review/
- **Physical objects as information channels between layers** — a code scrawled inside the in-game rulebook, gruesome table-won items that unlock cabin puzzles. *Why:* rewards handling and inspecting props; makes objects worth touching. https://entertainium.co/2021/10/18/inscryption-review/
- **Player-defaceable cards (carving/marking cards, permanent alteration)** — cards accrue history. *Why:* ownership through wear; a deck that shows what you did to it is *yours* in a way a fresh render isn't.
- **Cited as the exemplar of "horrific remediation"** — academic framing: embraces the tactile histories of analogue play while leveraging what only digital space can do. *Why:* the design thesis is hybrid, not imitation. https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2474/2467/2503
- **Evidence of tactile success:** the fan community has built physical replicas — brass bells, bones, working scales, candles in box sets — reverse-engineering the digital props back into objects. https://crypticcrafter.com/products/inscryption-full-box-set

## Solitaire (Microsoft / Windows) and descendants

- **The win cascade** — on victory, cards fall off each foundation stack and bounce down the screen leaving trails, using trivially simple physics: constant horizontal velocity, gravity on vertical, damped bounce at the floor. *Why:* the reward is a *toy that plays itself*, disproportionate to the achievement; it converts a terminal state into a spectacle, and it costs almost nothing to implement. https://github.com/zeke/solitaire · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Solitaire
- **Intermittent, escalating payoff structure** — "You don't win that often, but you have these incremental victories. When you do get the jackpot, or cards bouncing on the screen, it's super-exciting." *Why:* the cascade is the top of a reward ladder whose lower rungs (card flips, auto-completes) are constant. https://www.npr.org/2008/05/19/90583335/why-solitaire-is-still-the-game-to-beat
- **Historical note on scale/canonicity** — written by intern Wes Cherry, shipped with Windows 3.0 in 1990, played by 400M+ people; the bounce is now a meme and cultural shorthand for "win." *Why for synthesis:* this is the single most widely-shared physical-card-feedback reference in existence; players already know this vocabulary. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Solitaire · https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cards-when-you-win-in-solitaire
- **Drag-and-drop with card-follows-cursor and snap-back on invalid drop** — the original established the grammar: pick up, drag a ghost/stack, illegal drop springs the card home. *Why:* snap-back is a *negative* feedback affordance delivered physically instead of as an error message.

## Slay the Spire

- **Fan-shaped hand with per-card hover lift out of the fan** — cards arc along a fan curve, hovering pulls one forward and upright. *Why:* the fan is how a real hand is held; it also solves overlap/legibility while preserving the "held cards" read. https://interfaceingame.com/games/slay-the-spire/
- **Motion streaks on draw and discard** — small trails on cards entering/leaving hand. *Why:* explicitly praised as both pretty *and* functional — it communicates the flow/direction of the game state, so the player tracks card economy peripherally. https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2018/2/20/flash-thoughts-slay-the-spires-ui
- **Fully interruptible animations — no waiting on juice** — you can play cards as fast as you want and the animations run at their own pace behind you; you can even cast mid-draw. *Why:* this is the critical counterweight to juice — feedback must never gate input. Expert players get speed, new players get the show. https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2018/2/20/flash-thoughts-slay-the-spires-ui
- **Cast zone alignment + oversized hit targets + deliberate enemy spacing** — non-targeted cards need only be flicked upward; targeted cards have generous, well-separated drop targets. *Why:* reduces friction between intention and result; a mis-drop in a card game is uniquely infuriating because it's usually irreversible. https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2018/2/20/flash-thoughts-slay-the-spires-ui
- **Exaggerated hit VFX and audio relative to actual numbers** — impact conveyed with weight beyond what the damage figure warrants. *Why:* classic juice — response magnitude exceeds input magnitude so actions feel significant. https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2018/2/20/flash-thoughts-slay-the-spires-ui
- **Icon state changes at thresholds** (e.g. enemy intent/weapon icons shifting at damage tiers) — *Why:* danger becomes a shape you recognize, not a number you compute; offloads arithmetic to pattern recognition. https://www.cloudfallstudios.com/blog/2018/2/20/flash-thoughts-slay-the-spires-ui
- **Ethereal card burn-away animation** — specifically called out as satisfying. *Why:* mechanically-meaningful destruction rendered as a physical event (the card is consumed), so a rules keyword becomes a tactile memory.

## Monster Train

- **Navigation-light UI across a 3-floor vertical board** — praised for letting players focus on strategy without cumbersome navigation, despite a much denser board than Slay the Spire's. *Why:* the relevant lesson is negative — physicality budget spent on legibility first; juice that obstructs board reading is a net loss on a busy board. https://www.ninjarefinery.com/quick-look-monster-train/

## Marvel Snap

- **Accelerometer/gyroscope-driven 3D parallax on rare-tier cards (mobile)** — tilting the *device* tilts the card art layers. *Why:* the input is the player's hand moving in real space, which is the closest a screen gets to actual physical handling; it makes the card feel like it's behind glass in your palm rather than on a screen. https://seasonedgaming.com/2022/10/19/review-marvel-snap-the-art-of-the-cards/ · https://attackofthefanboy.com/guides/marvel-snap-upgrade-tiers-explained-frame-break-3d-animated-and-more/
- **Escalating visual-quality tiers as the progression currency** — flat art → frame break (art breaks out of the card frame) → 3D effect → animated/epic → higher tiers. *Why:* the entire meta-economy is denominated in *card physicality*; players grind for tactility itself, which is the strongest possible statement that physicality is a felt value. https://attackofthefanboy.com/guides/marvel-snap-upgrade-tiers-explained-frame-break-3d-animated-and-more/
- **Frame break specifically** — the artwork spills outside the card border. *Why:* breaks the plane, implying the card has depth and the frame is a real window; the cheapest possible "this object is not flat" signal.
- **Infinity Splits: Finish × Flare combinatorics** — Finish (card background material: Prism = sharp reflective rainbow hexagons replacing the art background, Foil = silvery rainbow, Ink, Gold) crossed with Flare (border/particle effect: Glimmer, Tone, Stardust, Krackle). *Why:* material + particle as independent axes yields unique-feeling objects; randomized combinations create personal, non-fungible copies — ownership through variance. https://marvelsnapzone.com/infinity-splits/ · https://gamerant.com/marvel-snap-infinity-splits-ink-gold-explained-guide/
- **Per-card custom animation, board effects, audio cues, and voice lines**, with VFX/sound built in-house and animation clips authored to be recombined per scenario. *Why:* identity per card; a card you've played 200 times has a signature sound you'd recognize blind. https://mobilegamer.biz/second-dinner-reveals-the-secrets-of-marvel-snaps-onboarding-and-card-design/
- **Half-tone dot motif throughout the visual language** — comic-print texture as the material metaphor. *Why:* the "material" of the whole UI is printed paper, so cards belong to a substance. https://mobilegamer.biz/second-dinner-reveals-the-secrets-of-marvel-snaps-onboarding-and-card-design/
- **Prototyped first with physical cards** before any digital build. *Why:* design provenance — the physical handling was the source, not a skin applied afterward. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/why-second-dinner-first-prototyped-marvel-snap-using-physical-cards
- **Note on lineage:** Ben Brode (Hearthstone game director through 2018) leads Second Dinner — the tactility philosophy is directly inherited. https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/how-are-marvel-snap-cards-designed-ben-brode-breaks-down-dev-process-1983450/

## MTG Arena (mostly cautionary)

- **Heavy per-card combat/spell animations** — modern VFX, expansions simultaneous with paper. *Why it's cited both ways:* players report "actions are followed by confusing animations and you lose the total overview" — juice that obscures board state actively harms a game whose skill ceiling is information tracking. https://steamcommunity.com/app/2141910/discussions/0/4330853446699117296/
- **Contrast case: MTG Online** — every format, zero combat animation, early-2000s UI. *Why:* the two products bracket the tradeoff; neither extreme is beloved, which is the useful data point. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Arena

## Digital board game adaptations (Wingspan, Root, and the general critique)

- **What's reported lost:** "the tactile pleasure of handling beautiful components, the social energy of a shared physical space, and the table presence of a well-produced game are impossible to replicate digitally" — cited most sharply for games whose appeal *is* physical (Azul's chunky tiles, dexterity games). https://www.boardgameserial.com/blog/board-game-digital-adaptations
- **Wingspan digital — the specific failure is information layout, not fidelity** — the physical board shows everything at once; digital splits player boards into scrollable views, and the compact view can't show nest style and ability info simultaneously. *Why it matters:* the loss framed as "tactility" is often really the loss of **at-a-glance parallel information**. Physical tables are wide; screens are deep. https://www.bigbossbattle.com/wingspan-is-as-relaxing-as-ever-but-the-digital-interface-clips-its-wings-slightly/
- **Wingspan digital — what survived:** pastel visuals, ambient music, the relaxing pace; reviewers rate it as one of the rare adaptations held in comparable regard to its tabletop original. *Why:* atmosphere and pacing carried the "feel" even where handling didn't. https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/10/30/wingspan-review/ · https://gideonsgaming.com/wingspan-digital-edition-european-expansion-review/
- **Root digital** — praised as extremely faithful, functioning as a low-cost trial of the physical game. *Why:* faithfulness to *rules and look* buys tolerance for reduced handling.
- **What digital wins on, per players:** no shuffling a giant deck, automatic scoring, no setup/teardown, playable solo at any hour. *Why:* the correct trade is to automate the *chores* of physicality while preserving the *pleasures* — the failure mode is automating both. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1054490/discussions/0/3043857610267528094/
- **Academic framing (Dislocated Boardgames)** — in board gaming, physical materials imply function so tightly the two can be understood as "material induced interactions"; the identified design opportunity is translating **physicalities not directly related to in-game function** — epistemic actions, fidgeting, the visible presence and physicality of another person. *Why:* this is the strongest theoretical support for building non-functional tactile affordances on purpose. https://doi.org/10.3390/mti3040072

## Design writing on juice and game feel

- **"Juice It or Lose It" — Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho, GDC Europe 2012** — the founding talk; large amounts of audiovisual feedback contribute directly to positive player experience, demonstrated by cranking a boring Breakout clone to eleven live on stage. Techniques: screen shake, freeze frames, audio feedback, camera work, exaggeration. *Why:* establishes that juice is not cosmetic — it intensifies the gameplay experience itself. https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose · https://gamejuice.co.uk/resources/juice-it-or-lose-it
- **"The Art of Screenshake" — Jan Willem Nijman, Vlambeer** — ~30 concrete tips, iteratively applied to one live prototype. Key ones transferable to cards: **hit-stop / action freezing** (pause 1–3 frames on an impactful event to create buildup), **permanence** (things that happened leave residue — shells, corpses, smoke — so the space remembers), **dynamic camera** responding to player facing, characterful idle/walk animation. *Why:* small individually-invisible details compound into the difference between two mechanically identical games. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkgkIXZ_13Y
- **"Don't Juice It or Lose It" (GDC counter-talk) / "Indies, resist the urge to juice it"** — the explicit pushback. *Why to include:* juice is routinely over-applied; feedback that delays input or obscures state is a regression. Pairs with Slay the Spire's interruptible animations as the practical resolution. https://gdcvault.com/play/1020861/Don-t-Juice-It-or · https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-
- **Steve Swink, *Game Feel*** — defines game feel as "real-time control of virtual objects in a simulated space, with interactions emphasised by polish," decomposed into six categories: **input, response, context, polish, metaphor, rules**. Juice = excessive feedback relative to input, so actions feel significant and results are *unambiguously predictable*. Hard threshold: real-time response means a correction cycle under **100ms**. Five experiences great-feeling games deliver: aesthetic sensation of control, pleasure of mastering a skill, extension of the senses, extension of identity, interaction with a unique physical reality. *Why:* "extension of identity" and "unique physical reality" are the precise vocabulary for what tactile card handling is doing. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-feel-the-secret-ingredient · https://lizengland.com/blog/review-game-feel-by-steve-swink/
- **"Good Game Feel: An Empirically Grounded Framework for Juicy Design" — Hicks, Dickinson, Holopainen, Gerling, DiGRA 2018** — survey of 17 developers, affinity-diagrammed into an analytical framework, then validated against two commercial games. *Why:* the only rigorous attempt to make juiciness analyzable rather than vibes; useful as a checklist structure. https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/936
- **Skeuomorphism literature** — textures, shadows and gradients create depth and realism so users grasp function without learning; resemblance to familiar physical items eases navigation; skeuomorphic references produce nostalgia and emotional connection. Cautionary parallel finding: in digital gambling interfaces, skeuomorphic elements measurably increase players' **illusion of control** over outcomes. *Why:* physical-feeling interfaces don't just feel better, they change perceived agency — powerful and ethically loaded. https://www.figma.com/resource-library/what-is-skeuomorphism/ · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342937796_The_Effect_of_Skeuomorphic_Digital_Interfaces_on_the_Illusion_of_Control_over_Gambling_Outcomes
- **"Game feel on the web: squash, shake, and the art of juice"** — web-specific application of the above (squash/stretch, shake, easing). https://valdemird.com/blog/game-feel-on-the-web/

## Implementation references (concrete, transferable)

- **Lever-arm torque from grab point** — `rotationForce = -mouseVelocity.x * rotationZSensitivity * leverArm`; spin scales with how far from center you grabbed. *Why:* this single detail is what separates "card follows cursor" from "card is being held." https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Rotate around the actual grab point, not the card center** — pivot set dynamically to cursor position on mousedown. *Why:* rotation about a fixed center reads as an animated sprite; rotation about the pinch point reads as paper held between fingers. https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Framerate-independent exponential damping** — `Mathf.Exp(-angularDamping * deltaTime)` on angular velocity. *Why:* gives smooth settle without frame-rate-dependent jitter; naive per-frame multiplication breaks on high-refresh displays. https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Hover = scale up + lift + tilt toward cursor**, computed from normalized cursor position within the card's bounds. https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Ghost/virtual insertion preview during drag** — the hand computes and shows in real time where the card would land, with layout modes including a trigonometric hand-fan. *Why:* removes drop uncertainty while keeping the physical drag. https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Holographic shader is cheap layering, not expensive math** — grayscale mask texture + UV-scrolled noise + position-and-time-driven hue rotation. Stated explicitly: "UI shaders don't need to be expensive." https://itch.io/blog/1325095/ui-physics-based-drag-drop-system-with-holographic-card-shaders-in-unity
- **Web spring-easing shorthand** — `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` as the standard overshoot/snap-back curve; but CSS transitions cannot convincingly simulate mass and inertia *in response to dragging*, so drag specifically wants JS-driven physics (GSAP InertiaPlugin-style velocity handoff with snap-to "notches"). *Why:* the split matters — declarative easing for state transitions, real physics for held objects. https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/InertiaPlugin/ · https://kinetics.colorion.co/
- **Velocity-threshold flick-to-throw** — dragging past a velocity threshold sends the card flying off with rotation derived from drag direction rather than snapping back. https://medium.com/@vitor_pontual/demystifying-the-react-spring-cards-stack-animation-c92026431448
- **Solitaire cascade physics, minimal version** — canvas, constant horizontal velocity, gravity on Y, damping coefficient on floor bounce. https://github.com/zeke/solitaire
- **Card sound design characteristics** — a card flip is a short swish plus a light tap; the deal/flip signature is a sharp transient followed by a **cardstock snap**, crisp and lightweight, simulating high-quality paper friction; a riffle shuffle is closer to a controlled waterfall than a percussion hit — fast, layered, full of micro-transients. Close-mic'd recordings are preferred for card-game UI because individual gestures must feel tactile. https://sounddino.com/en/effects/cards/ · https://sfxengine.com/sound-effects/card-game

## Design-principle takeaways

- **Feedback is the product, not the polish.** Strip Balatro's animation and sound and you have a spreadsheet; when a player repeats an action thousands of times, the feedback loop *is* the game. Budget it as core, not as a post-launch pass.
- **Springs, not lerps.** Overshoot, wobble and damped settle are the cheapest possible signal that an object has mass. Linear/ease-out motion reads as "UI"; spring settle reads as "thing." Hearthstone applied "bouncing and over-rotation" as a house rule to *all* UI, not just cards.
- **Rotate around the grab point and derive spin from cursor velocity.** This is the single highest-leverage detail separating "sprite following the mouse" from "paper held between fingers."
- **Neighbors must react.** A dragged card that pushes its siblings aside with inertia, then snaps in with magnetic damping, is what makes a row of cards read as a *hand*.
- **Encode state as elevation and material, not as labels.** Hover lift, selection lift, shadow depth, glow, and material (foil/glass/gold) carry mechanical meaning with zero reading cost.
- **Never let juice gate input.** Slay the Spire's fully interruptible animations — cast as fast as you like, cast mid-draw — is the resolution to the "Don't Juice It" critique. Also ship a juice/animation speed slider, as Balatro does.
- **Scale feedback intensity to event magnitude.** Screen shake in tiers, particle counts, audio layers — magnitude felt *before* the number is read. Shake as data, not as garnish.
- **Bind channels temporally.** Audio pitch synced to number-roll frequency, particle emission on the impact frame. Cross-modal events arriving together are perceived as one physical occurrence; staggered, they read as separate effects.
- **Add hit-stop.** A 1–3 frame freeze on impact costs nothing and creates the buildup that makes an event feel weighty (Nijman). Underused in card games specifically.
- **Build a deliberate fidget layer.** Hearthstone's stated rationale — players fiddle with things between turns in real life — is the clearest design argument in this corpus. Non-functional clickable toys convert dead opponent-turn time into play. Include a discoverable one (Pandaria's gong) to teach that the layer exists, and hide a long-tail secret in it.
- **Wear and history create ownership.** Inscryption's defaced cards, Marvel Snap's randomized Finish×Flare combinations, Hearthstone's goldens — a copy that is visibly *yours* is handled differently than a rendered instance.
- **Physicality is the strongest progression currency available.** Marvel Snap denominates its entire meta-economy in card tactility (frame break → 3D → animated). Players grind for feel.
- **Do what paper can't, but start from paper.** Hearthstone: cards feel like held cards, then become creatures that move, collide and crumble. Marvel Snap prototyped physically first. Inscryption is "horrific remediation" — analogue histories plus digital impossibility. Pure simulation of cardboard is the weakest option.
- **Automate the chores of physicality, preserve the pleasures.** Shuffling, scoring, setup and teardown are what players are glad to lose; handling, at-a-glance layout, and table presence are what they mourn. Most failed adaptations automate both.
- **The "lost tactility" complaint is often really lost parallel information.** Wingspan's actual sin is scrollable split views replacing a wide board you could read at a glance. Screens are deep; tables are wide. Solve legibility before adding feel — Monster Train's dense board is praised for navigation, not juice.
- **Design non-functional physicality on purpose.** The academic framing (Dislocated Boardgames): the design opportunity is translating physicalities *unrelated to in-game function* — epistemic fiddling, and the visible physical presence of the other player.
- **Tune by feel, not by math.** LocalThunk's picture-frame principle: a frame that feels level but isn't beats one technically level that feels askew. Perceptual tuning wins over correct values.
- **Respect the 100ms budget.** Swink: real-time response requires a correction cycle under 100ms. Any feel work that pushes input-to-first-visible-response past that is a net loss regardless of how good the animation is.
- **Reuse the shared vocabulary.** Solitaire's bouncing win cascade is known to ~400M people and has become a meme. Snap-back on invalid drop, riffle shuffle audio, the card slam — these are pre-learned; leaning on them is free onboarding.
- **Sound carries the material.** A card is a sharp transient plus a cardstock snap; a shuffle is a layered micro-transient waterfall. Close-mic'd, per-gesture audio does as much tactile work as any shader.
- **Ethical note.** Skeuomorphic interfaces measurably increase perceived *illusion of control* over outcomes (gambling research). Tactility makes players feel more causally responsible than they are — worth knowing when the game involves randomness or monetization.
